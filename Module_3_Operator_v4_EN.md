# Module 3 — The Matter Operator T_M (Version 4.2)

## 3.1 Purpose of the operator

The operator **T_M** describes how matter changes the properties of a
Hexaphoton. It is **not new physics**, but a **compact notation** for
established classical transformations: refraction, reflection,
scattering, polarization change, absorption.

T_M unifies the Jones, Mueller, Fresnel and Snell formalisms into a
single notation.

> **T_M is not a memory.** It is a pure pass-through rule — wave in,
> changed wave out. The "shape" of a scattering object lies in *how* T_M
> redirects the directions, not in a stored image.

---

## 3.2 Definition of the operator

A Hexaphoton is \( H = (f, P, d) \). The matter operator acts:

\[
T_M(H) = (f', P', d')
\]

with \( f' = f \) (linear optics), \( P' = M_P\,P \) (polarization
matrix), \( d' = M_d\,d \) (change of direction).

---

## 3.3 The three effects on incident light

Every real T_M is a mixture of three basic effects.

### Redirecting — the Hexaphoton continues to exist
Reflection and refraction change the direction \( d \); frequency and
polarization are preserved (transformed in an ordered way where
applicable). Information is preserved.
Examples: Snell refraction, Fresnel reflection, Mie/Rayleigh scattering.

### Selecting — part passes through, part does not
Apertures and slits let only part of the incident Hexaphotons pass; the
rest is blocked. This is a **selection** of the Hexafield — the mechanism
behind diffraction at a slit and the pinhole camera.

> The slit effect is classical wave optics (it works with sound waves
> too). It is **not** the quantum-mechanical measurement process.

### Absorbing — energy transfers, the light's order dissolves
The Hexaphoton is taken up by the material; its energy passes into the
disordered thermal motion of the atoms. The energy is conserved — the
ordered light structure (direction, polarization, phase, sharp
frequency) is afterwards no longer recoverable within the material.

---

## 3.4 Absorption — formal description

Absorption is described by a damping factor \( \alpha \in [0,1] \):

\[
T_M(H) = \alpha \cdot (f, P', d')
\]

- \( \alpha = 1 \): no absorption
- \( \alpha = 0 \): complete absorption
- \( 0 < \alpha < 1 \): partial absorption

The damping factor \( \alpha \) depends on the material and on the
frequency — an object can be transparent for one colour and opaque for
another. This frequency dependence is the reason materials have colours.

---

## 3.5 Emission — two kinds

Emission is **not** described by T_M; it creates a new Hexaphoton:

\[
\emptyset \;\longrightarrow\; H_{\text{emit}} = (f_{\text{emit}}, P_{\text{emit}}, d_{\text{emit}})
\]

There are **two physically distinct** kinds of emission:

**Thermal radiation (glowing) — temperature-determined.** Every warm body
emits light because it is warm. The colour distribution depends **only on
the temperature**, not on the material (Planck's radiation law):
~800 °C red, ~1200 °C yellow-orange, ~1500 °C almost white. A broad,
continuous spectrum.

**Spectral lines — material-determined.** Emission at sharp,
material-specific frequencies (sodium yellow, neon red-orange). The
frequencies correspond to the absorption pattern of the same material —
two sides of the same characteristic frequency pattern.

> Emitted Hexaphotons are **new** Hexaphotons. Their properties are
> determined by the temperature or the atomic species — not by
> previously absorbed Hexaphotons. The speculative counter-position is
> documented separately in the hypothesis collection (H2, H3).

---

## 3.6 Energy balance

\[
E_{\text{in}} = E_{\text{redirected}} + E_{\text{transmitted}} + E_{\text{absorbed}}
\]

The absorbed energy is heat in the material — not lost. What is lost
(dissolved) is only the *ordered information* of the absorbed share.

---

## 3.7 Nonlinear extensions (optional)

For later work, an extended operator **T\*_M** can be defined:

\[
T^*_M(H) = (f + \Delta f,\; P',\; d')
\]

It describes Raman and Brillouin scattering, generation of new
frequencies (SHG, THG), the Kerr effect, intensity-dependent refraction.
T\*_M is **not part** of the core system.

---

## 3.8 System boundary

What the matter does with the heat after absorption — expand, rise
through lower density (convection), melt — lies **outside** Hexaphotonics.
That is thermodynamics and fluid mechanics. Hexaphotonics ends at "energy
has arrived as heat in the material".

---

## 3.9 Decoupling from speculative hypotheses

Speculative ideas from the hypothesis collection (H1–H7, e.g. survival of
absorbed photons, inner rotation, heat as stored photons) are **not**
described by T_M. The operator maps exclusively classical,
experimentally confirmed transformations.

---

## 3.10 Summary

The operator T_M:

- is a compact notation of classical optics
- preserves the frequency (linear optics)
- changes polarization and direction
- acts in three ways: redirecting, selecting, absorbing
- describes absorption via a damping factor
- creates **no** new photons (emission is a separate process)
- is fully compatible with Maxwell optics
- remains free of speculative assumptions

Optional extensions (T\*_M) enable nonlinear effects without changing the
core.
