# Module 1 — The Hexaphoton (Version 4.1)

## 1.1 Definition

A **Hexaphoton** is a single light element (an idealized mode), described
by exactly **three independent degrees of freedom**:

- **Frequency** \( f \)
- **Polarization** \( P \)
- **Direction** \( d \)

These three quantities are the **primary quantities** of the Hexaphoton.

All further quantities are **derived**:

- Energy \( E = h f \)
- Momentum \( \vec{p} = \frac{h f}{c}\, \vec{d} \)
- Phase \( \phi \) — *not a property of a single Hexaphoton*

> **Naming.** The prefix "Hexa" (six) bundles the six quantity types
> \((E, f, P, \phi, \vec{p}, \vec{d})\) into a memorable label. It is
> **not a law of nature**: a single Hexaphoton has three independent
> degrees of freedom, not six.

---

## 1.2 Primary quantities

**Frequency \( f \)** — determines energy and colour; also fixes the
wavelength \( \lambda = c/f \). An independent quantity.

**Polarization \( P \)** — a pure Jones vector (two complex components,
normalized). An independent quantity. A single Hexaphoton is always
*purely* polarized; partial polarization is a Hexafield property.

**Direction \( d \)** — a unit vector in space, \( |\vec{d}| = 1 \). It
carries **direction, not position**. An independent quantity.

---

## 1.3 Derived quantities

### Energy \( E \)
\[
E = h f \qquad \text{(Constraint C1)}
\]

### Momentum \( \vec{p} \)
\[
\vec{p} = \frac{h f}{c}\, \vec{d} \qquad \text{(Constraint C2)}
\]

\( h \) is Planck's constant, \( c \) the speed of light. Energy and
momentum are fully determined by \( f \), respectively by \( f \) and
\( \vec{d} \); they are carried along for notational convenience but
are not independent information.

### Phase \( \phi \)
The phase is **not a property of a single Hexaphoton**. An absolute phase
of an isolated light element is physically meaningless — only phase
*differences* are observable. The phase is therefore a **relational
quantity**, defined only within the Hexafield:

\[
\Delta\phi_{ij} = \phi_i - \phi_j
\]

> **Origin of this definition.** That the phase is relational follows from
> classical wave optics itself (only phase differences govern
> interference) — it is **established physics**, not the import of a
> speculative hypothesis. The hypothesis collection (H1–H7) remains
> strictly separate from this module.

---

## 1.4 The Hexaphoton as a data structure

\[
H = (f, P, d)
\]

All other quantities are functions of these.

---

## 1.5 The Hexafield

A **Hexafield** is a set of \( N \) Hexaphotons — a source or an image:

\[
\mathcal{H} = \{ H_1, H_2, \dots, H_N \}
\]

Only within the Hexafield do the following arise:

- interference
- phase (as the relation \( \Delta\phi_{ij} \))
- coherence
- wave character
- partial polarization
- the reconstructable spatial position of the source

> **Clarification — the quantity types stay the same.** A Hexafield of
> \( N \) Hexaphotons does **not** have \( 6N \) "dimensions". The same
> six quantity types remain as fixed channels; more Hexaphotons mean more
> values within the same channels, not more channels.

---

## 1.6 Note on interpretation

The Hexaphoton is **not new physics**, but a **compact notation** for
classical wave optics.

Speculative ideas from the hypothesis collection (e.g. inner rotation,
survival of absorbed photons) are **not** integrated into Module 1; they
are deliberately kept separate.
